1. What this policy covers

This Privacy Policy describes WatchJam's app features and data practices. The app is built around invite-based rooms, synchronized playback, profiles, social features, chat, moderation controls, and media metadata. The disclosures below distinguish owner-confirmed settings from implementation details that have not been verified in the production release.

WatchJam uses Supabase Auth, PostgreSQL, and Realtime. The owner has confirmed that the configured production Supabase project is hosted in Singapore (ap-southeast-1) on the Free plan, with email/password and Google sign-in, new sign-ups, and email confirmation enabled; anonymous sign-in and manual account linking are disabled. The exact production project-to-build mapping, enabled Storage services, OAuth scopes, subprocessors, processing outside Singapore, production logs, backups, and provider retention have not been established by this review. This policy does not claim that all processing occurs in Singapore. The app includes an official YouTube IFrame player path, but production YouTube availability is not verified here.

Age eligibility

WatchJam is strictly for people aged 18 and older. Users under 18 are not permitted to use WatchJam. The local app source includes an 18+ self-attestation gate before authentication and app routes are initialized. It stores only a local eligibility marker; it does not collect or store a date of birth or identity document for this check. This is self-attestation, not authoritative age verification.

2. Information the current app can handle

Account and identity

  • Email address and authentication credentials are handled through Supabase Auth when you use email/password sign-in or sign-up.
  • Google sign-in uses an OAuth flow. Information returned by Google, such as account email and provider profile fields made available to the app, can be used to establish the WatchJam account.
  • The first-release sign-in flow offers email/password and Google sign-in. The owner-confirmed production Auth configuration has anonymous sign-in and manual account linking disabled. A guest login path in source is not offered through the normal first-release flow; no guest identity or guest cleanup period is claimed.
  • A profile can contain a username, display name, avatar URL, and bio, plus server timestamps.

Room and social activity

  • Rooms can include a name, room code, host, visibility, lifecycle status, chat/voice settings, voting mode, and timestamps.
  • Membership and invites can include a user, role, membership state, join/leave/last-seen timestamps, invite expiry, usage limits, revocation state, and a protected invite token hash.
  • Friends, blocks, and moderation reports are represented as account-scoped records. An authenticated member can report a user or visible room message in the app. Reports can include a subject type, category, optional details, status, and timestamps.
  • Room chat messages can include the sender, message body, room, a one-way duplicate-detection fingerprint, and creation time. Room access and safety rules limit who can read or send them.
  • Block controls can hide account-scoped social and room results from the blocker. Room hosts and moderators can use server-authorized safety controls such as join locks, member removal, bans, and invite rotation.
  • Queue and playback state can include provider and media identifiers, titles/subtitles, duration, position, rate, playback status, votes, controller/member identifiers, and timestamps. The audited database contract does not store media bytes.
  • Private room Realtime channels can carry bounded playback/lifecycle hints and coarse presence such as user ID, display name, and a state like joining, ready, watching, or reconnecting.

Media metadata

  • The checked-in app contains an official YouTube IFrame player path. The default feature flag leaves it disabled, and production enablement is not verified here. If enabled, YouTube may receive information as part of serving its player under its own privacy policy.
  • For user-owned/direct media, the persistent database contract stores title, credential-free HTTPS source URI, MIME type, duration, and timestamps. Local device-file URIs remain local to the device in the audited implementation; the app does not upload media bytes through this path.

YouTube search and recent searches

When YouTube search is available in your build and you submit a search, WatchJam sends the search phrase and selected category in an authenticated request to its configured backend. The backend forwards the query to Google's YouTube Data API and returns video identifiers, titles, channel names, thumbnail URLs, and duration metadata where available. Google receives the search query and may process it under its own policies. See Google's Privacy Policy. If you select or queue a result, its video identifier and some metadata may be stored with room playback or queue data as described above.

The reviewed app source separates Privacy Policy agreement from Terms acceptance. For a signed-in persistent account, it checks the server-controlled active published policy version and URL and requires a separate affirmative agreement before exposing authenticated app routes. Signing in, viewing the notice, or accepting the Terms does not record Privacy Policy consent. The YouTube search function checks current Terms and Privacy Policy consent before contacting Google, and protected room and moderation RPCs use the shared legal-acceptance boundary. This describes source-level behavior only. The migration has not been deployed to production, and an active policy version and URL have not been configured in the production database. Production consent behavior has not been verified.

The reviewed source includes a server worker intended to refresh stored YouTube API metadata by day 25 or remove unavailable metadata before the 30-calendar-day temporary-storage limit, while preserving separate WatchJam room and queue coordination data. This is a source-level design only. The production migration, worker schedule, alerts, gateway/Edge Function logs, Google's request-log handling, and provider backup expiry have not been verified. This policy does not promise that refresh or deletion is active in production or that logs and backups contain no older copies.

The current search-function source does not explicitly write the query phrase to WatchJam's application database. That source-level fact does not establish what production infrastructure logs or retains.

The app stores up to 12 recent in-app YouTube search phrases per signed-in account in device-local SecureStore. You can remove an individual phrase or clear the recent-search list in the search screen. When an authenticated account-deletion request is recorded, the app attempts to remove that account's local YouTube search history; if local removal fails, the app shows a retry action. This local cleanup does not itself delete the WatchJam account or confirm that server-side deletion is complete.

Web Browser activity and website storage

The Android app includes a built-in Web Browser. It saves page URLs and titles, including URL query parameters where present, for up to 60 recent history entries and up to 40 bookmarks in an app-managed document file on the device. Search phrases entered in the address bar are sent to Google Search and may appear in the resulting URL saved in local history. The current Browser lets you clear local history and remove bookmarks. These lists are not sent to WatchJam's room database by the current Browser implementation.

The Android WebView configuration disables third-party cookies and DOM storage. The app does not currently provide controls to inspect or clear website cookies or all site data, and it does not claim that first-party cookies or other website state are disabled or removed when local history is cleared or an account-deletion request is made. Websites you visit may receive connection and page request information and handle information you submit under their own terms and privacy notices. Their practices are separate from WatchJam's local history and bookmark lists.

Device and diagnostic information

  • The checked-in Android configuration requests INTERNET and blocks notification, storage, overlay, and vibration permissions. The audited build does not implement microphone capture, location collection, camera capture, or contacts import.
  • Push notification delivery is unconfigured. Notification preferences are local boolean settings with opt-in defaults; the current delivery adapter does not request permission or register a push token.
  • The default telemetry sink is a no-op. The optional in-memory diagnostic sink is bounded and process-local. The audited code does not wire an analytics exporter, advertising SDK, or durable telemetry queue.
  • The app stores a local 18+ eligibility marker and a versioned local Terms-acceptance marker on the device. These markers do not contain a date of birth or identity document. For a persistent authenticated session, the prepared server boundary also records the accepted Terms version, timestamp, and source; that record is currently verified only in disposable local staging and is not a claim that production enforcement is deployed.

3. Why the app uses this information

The current code uses the information above to authenticate a session, show and edit a profile, create or join rooms, enforce membership and moderation rules, synchronize playback, show room presence, manage queues and chat, support friends and blocks, submit reports, and display media through an approved player path.

4. When information is shared

  • Supabase. WatchJam uses Supabase for authentication, database, and Realtime. The owner has confirmed the configured project region is Singapore (ap-southeast-1); this does not mean all processing occurs there. Production Storage, subprocessors, cross-border routing, logs, and backup settings have not been established by this review.
  • Google. Google participates in sign-in when you choose Google sign-in and receives information needed for that flow. Exact production OAuth scopes and provider-side retention have not been verified here.
  • YouTube. YouTube may process search requests and player-related information when those features are enabled and used. Search queries and returned metadata are described in the YouTube section above. Production enablement and infrastructure logging have not been verified.
  • Other room members. People in a room may see room information made available to members, such as display names, coarse presence, chat, queue metadata, and playback state. Access is intended to be controlled by room visibility and membership rules.
  • Safety operations. The app source includes report, block, and room-safety controls. Production review operations and staffing have not been verified; this policy does not promise a response time or outcome.

The advertising, analytics, and paid-feature configuration of a future production build has not been verified. This policy does not claim that those features are present or absent; if they are introduced, this policy should be updated before they are offered.

5. Security and storage choices

The Android app source uses an operating-system-backed SecureStore adapter for native authentication sessions. The web adapter keeps session state in process memory. Row-level security, authenticated RPCs, and room membership checks are part of the database source contract; this does not establish the production service's deployed configuration or operating controls.

No security measure is perfect. Production access controls, incident response, provider logging, and backup procedures have not been verified for the production environment.

Account deletion status

The reviewed app source contains an in-app account-deletion request and status interface. That interface alone does not establish that a request reaches or is fulfilled by the production service; production request processing and deletion fulfillment have not been verified.

There is no operational external account-deletion service at this time. This website does not accept or complete deletion requests.

6. Retention

Future targets; not yet operational. If a verified production deletion process is later deployed, the approved targets are acknowledgment of verified requests within 72 hours and ordinary completion aimed within seven days. An approved legitimate exception may use a standard 30-day processing target. Cancellation is available until irreversible processing begins. These are future targets, not active service guarantees; no functioning production deletion route is available now.

The approved future deletion scope is intended to remove the Auth identity, profile, and associated account-owned data. User-hosted rooms may be closed after a clear warning, and room-owned data may be removed as part of the request. Unnecessary ended-room records are targeted for removal within 30 days. Support correspondence is targeted for 90 days after ticket closure unless an active case requires longer retention. Necessary security/moderation records and minimal deletion audit records may be retained for up to 12 months, and legally required records may be retained for the period required by applicable law. These are approved future policy targets, not current production capabilities.

Routine backup expiry is a future target of 30 days, subject to the provider's actual backup and restore capabilities. The production backup schedule has not been verified, and this is not an active system guarantee.

7. Your choices

  • You can sign out of the current session.
  • You can edit the profile fields exposed by the app, where the account is configured.
  • Guest or anonymous login is not available in the first release. Joining a Jam requires an email/password or Google account.
  • Authenticated room members can use in-app controls to report a user or visible message and to block another account. Guest or anonymous identities are not part of the first release.
  • An authenticated deletion request/status path and worker design are prepared locally, but account deletion is not live for public users or in production. The disposable local staging workflow is not a production deployment and does not make the deletion URL a completed deletion service. The separate account-deletion page is excluded from this phased package. Production deletion is not operational.
